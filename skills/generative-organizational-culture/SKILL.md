# Generative Organizational Culture

## DORA Research Context

Based on Dr. Ron Westrum's typology (2004, BMJ Quality & Safety). Google's Project Aristotle found that high-performing teams need trust, psychological safety, meaningful work, and clarity. The 2019 State of DevOps Report confirmed that psychological safety predicts software delivery performance, organizational performance, and productivity.

## What It Is

A high-trust, information-flow-optimizing culture that contrasts with pathological (power-oriented) and bureaucratic (rule-oriented) cultures. Generative culture is predictive of software delivery performance and organizational performance.

## Problem Solved

- Bad news is hidden or punished; problems fester until they become crises
- Blame culture prevents learning from failures
- Information is hoarded in silos
- Innovation is stifled; "we've always done it this way"

## Key Practices

1. **High cooperation:** Create cross-functional teams with shared responsibility for building, deploying, and maintaining the product.
2. **Train the messengers:** Hold blameless postmortems; create an environment where anyone can surface problems anytime.
3. **Share risks:** Encourage shared responsibility for quality, availability, reliability, and security.
4. **Encourage bridging:** Break down silos; co-locate ops with dev; include ops throughout the delivery lifecycle.
5. **Let failure lead to inquiry:** Respond to failures by asking what caused them and how to prevent recurrence.
6. **Implement novelty:** Encourage experimentation; give engineers weekly exploration time; reward process improvements.

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Fails | What To Do Instead |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Ignoring culture entirely | Technical work is treated as purely technology/process challenge | Recognize culture as predictive of performance |
| Focusing only on local team cultures | Ignoring broader organizational dynamics | Understand how team cultures interact with the wider org |
| Punishing bad news | Problems stay hidden until they're crises | Blameless postmortems; reward messengers |
| Not encouraging novelty | Stagnation; "we've always done it this way" | Allow and reward experimentation |

## How to Measure

Survey on 7-point Likert scale (unlabeled, untitled items):
- **Information is actively sought:** Do people go looking for what they need to know?
- **Messengers are not punished for bad news:** Can anyone raise concerns without fear?
- **Responsibilities are shared:** Is there collective ownership?
- **Cross-functional collaboration is encouraged and rewarded:** Do teams work across boundaries?
- **Failures are treated as opportunities to improve the system:** Is inquiry the default response?
- **New ideas are welcomed:** Does the team embrace novelty?

## Coaching Patterns

1. **Start with blameless postmortems:** This single practice shifts culture toward inquiry over blame.
2. **Model the behavior from leadership:** Respond well to bad news; ask "what can we learn?" not "whose fault?"
3. **Create cross-functional teams:** Shared responsibility for outcomes breaks down silos.
