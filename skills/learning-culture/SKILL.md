# Learning Culture

## DORA Research Context

DORA research shows learning culture is a significant predictor of software delivery performance, improving deployment frequency, lead time, MTTR, and change failure rate. It also strengthens team culture. Cross-disciplinary research (accounting) corroborates that a climate for learning is predictive of performance gains.

## What It Is

An organizational culture that values learning — treating it as strategic investment, not grudging necessity — which directly contributes to software delivery performance.

## Problem Solved

- Teams repeat the same mistakes because failures aren't analyzed
- No time or budget for training, conferences, or exploration
- Knowledge is hoarded; there's no mechanism for sharing
- Failure is punished, so people avoid taking smart risks

## Key Practices

1. **Create a training budget and advocate for it internally:** Put resources behind formal education.
2. **Make it safe to fail:** Treat failures as learning opportunities; hold blameless postmortems.
3. **Create opportunities and spaces to share information:** Weekly lightning talks, monthly lunch-and-learns.
4. **Make resources available for continued education:** Conference attendance, certifications, study groups.
5. **When people attend conferences, have them share:** Presentations, trip reports, mini-conferences.

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Fails | What To Do Instead |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Viewing learning as an expense | Training gets cut first in budget tightening | Reframe as strategic investment necessary for growth |
| Punishing failure | People hide mistakes; organization can't improve | Treat failures as learning; blameless postmortems |
| No dedicated time or budget | Learning never happens | Create training budget; set aside exploration time |

## How to Measure

Survey on: "Learning is the key to improvement," "Once we quit learning we endanger our future," "Learning is viewed as an investment, not an expense."

## Coaching Patterns

1. **Start a training budget:** Even a small one signals that learning matters.
2. **Create a knowledge-sharing cadence:** Weekly lightning talks are low-barrier and high-reward.
3. **Model learning from failure:** Leaders should share their own mistakes and what they learned.

## Stop and Clarify Pattern

### When to Stop
- Requirements are ambiguous or contradictory
- The codebase contradicts what you expected
- The task is bigger than it initially seemed
- You're about to build something based on an assumption you haven't validated
- A 30-second question now saves hours of rework

### How to Clarify
1. State what you understand the task to be
2. Identify the specific ambiguity or issue
3. Ask a specific, answerable question
4. Wait for the answer before continuing

This is a learning behavior — catching misunderstandings early prevents building the wrong thing.
